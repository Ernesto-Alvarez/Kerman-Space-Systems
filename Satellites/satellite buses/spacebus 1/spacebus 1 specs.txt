The SpaceBus-1 satellite bus

***Description***

SpaceBus-1 is a modular satellite bus architecture that can be used to create satellite and probes in a consistent and reliable way.
The system consists of a computer unit, one or more power units and a propulsion unit.

***Parts***

**Computer unit**
The SpaceBus-1 computer unit is based on the OKTO computer, with dual Communotron-16 radios for control. The rationale for using the OKTO computer instead of more advanced models is that (while SAS is desirable) more advanced capabilities are not necessary. Satellites need stabilisation, but all other operations can be done manually (as there usually is enough time to do them) or the RemoteTech flight computer can do them. These extended capabilities are not worth the extra cost and energy use. Moreover, an eight-sided computer is a better radial support for additional systems than a six-sided one.

The computer unit has 6 free sides that can be used for mission payloads.

**Power unit**
The SpaceBus-1 power unit consists of a ring of solar panels with batteries for EC storage. All power units have a 400 EC capacity provided by two Z-200 battery banks. Type 1 is the basic unit and consists of a ring of 6 panels in a hexagonal configuration. Other variants include a 3 panel unit (type 2) and a 4 panel unit (type 3). Types 2 and 3 have the panels more recessed and have a smaller diameter as a consequence. A single power unit is capable of powering the computer unit, provided average power is generated.

*Implementation notes*
When using multiple power units, offset the units to smooth out power fluctuations.

**Propulsion unit**

The SpaceBus-1 propulsion unit consists of a small (2KN) rocket with either 1 or two OSCAR-B tanks. The surface of the tanks can be used to mount mission payloads, and can hold between 6 to 8 (type 1) or 6 to 16 (type 2) items. There is no formal definition of hardpoints in the propulsion unit.
